# Human campaign testimony

This history covers the 1998 PC game *Star Wars: Rebellion*, called *Star Wars:
Supremacy* in the United Kingdom. Sources were admitted only when their title,
mechanics, units, characters, or victory conditions identify that game. The
contemporary [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/star-wars-rebellion-review/1900-2532756/)
explicitly confirms the regional title.

The record is uneven. Guides document repeated play but usually give advice,
not telemetry. Forum posts provide firsthand incidents but small samples.
Pander's illustrated campaign provides exact game days from 0 to 1177, but the
pages do not explicitly identify whether the opponent was a human or the
computer. It is therefore used for campaign chronology, not as direct evidence
of AI logic.
[Pander campaign index](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/),
[campaign setup](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%201/).

## Early campaign: survey, disperse, persuade, and build

The guides agree that the opening is action-dense even when fleet combat is
not. MasterVodo recommends pausing on day one to map friendly and enemy
production, identify sabotage targets, move every character, activate every
facility, strengthen vulnerable sectors, and begin reconnaissance.
[MasterVodo guide](https://members.tripod.com/buds_korner/strategy/vodo.htm).

Farrell assigns early priority to diplomacy, recruitment, and espionage. He
recommends keeping characters and facilities occupied, using the Emperor for
early recruitment, and delaying early blockades while fleets remain vulnerable.
[Farrell guide, General Strategy and faction sections](https://the-spoiler.com/STRATEGY/Lucasarts/rebellion.2.html).

Borelli's *Supremacy* walkthrough describes a randomized opening in which even
the Empire may feel outnumbered. It recommends simultaneous diplomatic
expansion and defensive construction: fighters first for the Alliance, smaller
capital ships first for the Empire, with construction yards used to accelerate
the industrial base. Premature forceful occupation can trigger uprisings that
consume troops and characters.
[PC Zone walkthrough mirror](https://www.cheatbook.de/wfiles/starwsso.htm).

The GOG-preserved memo gives the Alliance a more specific opening: disperse
from exposed Yavin, keep Mon Mothma recruiting, send Leia to diplomacy, use
Wedge for ship research when shipyards permit, and rely heavily on characters
and special forces before industry can produce decisive fleets.
[GOG strategy discussion](https://www.gog.com/forum/star_wars_rebellion/strategy_tips_for_swrebellion_part_one).

Pander's campaign shows why the expansion race matters. Around day 103, new
construction and troops were constrained by processed materials, so exploration
and acquisition of mines and refineries became an immediate concern.
[Pander, days 101 to 128](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%208/).

## Middle campaign: specialize, gather intelligence, and create fronts

Rogue describes loyalty as an operating resource, not decoration. Diplomacy and
successful political missions reduce garrison burdens, while unsupported forced
control can cause an uprising, halt production, damage personnel, and eventually
flip a system. Colonization expands resources and creates new production sites.
[Rogue guide, loyalty, uprising, and colonization sections](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/198776-star-wars-rebellion/faqs/19754).

Players specialize production worlds because one low-level facility is slow.
The GOG memo recommends concentrated construction, training, and shipyard
worlds and warns that consuming refined materials faster than they arrive can
stall every queue. Borelli likewise ties sustained expansion to mines,
refineries, maintenance headroom, and protected resource worlds.
[GOG strategy discussion](https://www.gog.com/forum/star_wars_rebellion/strategy_tips_for_swrebellion_part_one),
[PC Zone walkthrough mirror](https://www.cheatbook.de/wfiles/starwsso.htm).

Fleet doctrine becomes combined-arms. Borelli recommends one or two powerful
groups with capital ships, screens, full fighter complements, transports, and
leaders, plus fast local forces against raids. Farrell likewise recommends
consolidation and treats early fleets as mobile mission platforms before the
main killing force is ready.
[PC Zone walkthrough mirror](https://www.cheatbook.de/wfiles/starwsso.htm),
[Farrell guide](https://the-spoiler.com/STRATEGY/Lucasarts/rebellion.2.html).

Blockades are operational tools rather than ends in themselves. They interrupt
resource movement, stop production or reinforcements, create zero-travel mission
bases, and prepare bombardment or invasion. A successful assault still needs
transport capacity, ground forces, orbital control, and enough occupation force
to manage loyalty.
[PC Zone walkthrough mirror](https://www.cheatbook.de/wfiles/starwsso.htm),
[GOG strategy discussion](https://www.gog.com/forum/star_wars_rebellion/strategy_tips_for_swrebellion_part_one),
[Farrell guide](https://the-spoiler.com/STRATEGY/Lucasarts/rebellion.2.html).

A human-versus-AI Empire campaign had reached an economic stall and active
fronts by day 444. The Rebel computer had raided systems, bounced fleets against
defenses, and massed toward Coruscant, while the Imperial player was coping with
refined-material shortages, long capital-ship production, sabotage, and the
discovery of a heavily defended Rebel headquarters.
[GameFAQs Empire campaign](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/198776-star-wars-rebellion/50893998).

## Late campaign: dismantle recovery, then hunt objectives

Pander's days 640 to 682 illustrate the transition from territorial competition
to infrastructure war. Mobile groups suppressed fighter garrisons, special
forces destroyed mines, refineries, and production, an interdictor trapped
ships, and troop transports prepared a ground assault on Coruscant.
[Pander, days 640 to 682](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%2031/).

By days 891 to 1009, the same campaign had become an intelligence and capture
operation. Longprobe Y-wings searched the Outer Rim, the Alliance headquarters
moved, blockades narrowed escape routes, and missions pursued Palpatine and
other officers after major Imperial fleets could no longer hold the field.
[Pander, days 891 to 1009](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%2037/).

After day 1010 there were no further major space engagements. The remaining
campaign centered on reconnaissance, insurgency, system isolation, and the
search for leaders. The Rebel victory sequence finally played on day 1177 after
the final operation against Vader.
[Pander, days 1010 to 1105](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%2038/),
[Pander, days 1106 to 1177](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%2039/).

This produces a recognizable late-game condition: material victory can precede
formal victory by a long interval because the winning side must still locate,
capture, and hold every selected objective. A veteran forum account similarly
describes capturing an AI-controlled Palpatine on a risky mission only after
Vader had been captured months earlier and Coruscant weeks earlier.
[FFG forum archive](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/186253-star-wars-rebellion-1998-pc-anyone-familiar-with-it/).

## Reported duration

One GameFAQs discussion reports roughly 300 to 400 days for a fast,
victory-focused game, day 800 for a deliberately divided galaxy, and about
1,000 days for a thorough style. The same thread estimates one to two hours of
real time per 100 days at very slow speed. These are individual play reports,
not population statistics.
[GameFAQs duration discussion](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/198776-star-wars-rebellion/50518406?page=2).

Pander's documented 150-system, Medium campaign ended at day 1177. Another
veteran set a goal of winning a small Hard game before day 1000. Together these
sources support a broad hundreds-to-low-thousands-of-days campaign scale, but
not a precise acceptance percentile.
[Pander setup](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%201/),
[Pander finale](https://lparchive.org/Star-Wars-Rebellion/Update%2039/),
[FFG forum archive](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/186253-star-wars-rebellion-1998-pc-anyone-familiar-with-it/).

## Human-versus-human contrast

Farrell recommends learning against the computer and then playing a human
because the computer opponent does not exercise the game's strategic depth.
A later GameFAQs review reaches the same conclusion: weak single-player play
does not prepare a player for competitive tactics. The reviewer separately
estimates that a campaign can take upwards of 20 hours, depending on playstyle.
[Farrell guide](https://the-spoiler.com/STRATEGY/Lucasarts/rebellion.2.html),
[GameFAQs user review](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/198776-star-wars-rebellion/reviews/121806).

Human strategy testimony therefore establishes an upper capability model, not
the exact original computer policy. It shows what the shared rules permit and
which operations form a coherent campaign. Direct computer-opponent evidence
is separated in [AI behavior and parity observables](ai-behavior-parity.md).
